The economics. Reading a million visits by hand, at the protocol's pace of about 2 minutes a chart, is 33,333 hours of a specialist's time. The software does the same million notes for tens to hundreds of dollars of computing, even for the largest models we priced; those are illustrative prices and assumed speeds, a dry run, not quotes. Proving the software is good enough is small: about 31 specialist hours, 233 charts each read by 4 reviewers. So the argument is not that computers are cheap. It is that specialist hours are the bottleneck, and the software can replace most of them, but only after it passes the acceptance test. If asked for a dollar figure: at an illustrative 100 dollars an hour, validation is about 3,100 dollars and reviewing everything by hand about 3.3 million. If asked about errors: the study also counts unsafe errors, such as missing severe disease, and reports them separately.
