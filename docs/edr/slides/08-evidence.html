The seven kinds of evidence

When the evidence disagrees, a fixed order decides

1st

Exam form

The checkbox, or the gum score (PSR) when it is not ticked.

2nd

Diagnosis codes

The ICD-10 code on the procedure, done or planned.

3rd

Chart note

A written diagnosis first; else the findings written in the note.

4th

Measurements

Attachment loss, pockets, bleeding, teeth lost, by the 2017 rules.

Ranked 24 ways, tested against the specialists: four sources can be ordered 4×3×2×1 = 24 ways; a ranking that agrees better replaces this one. Procedure codes are compared, not used.

The seven kinds of evidence are: the clinician's note; the exam form, with a gum score and a status checkbox; the procedure codes done and planned; the diagnosis codes on those procedures, done and planned; and the probing chart of gum measurements. Procedure codes are only a rough guess from the treatment, so they are compared, not used. The other four are used in a fixed order: exam form first, then diagnosis codes, then the note, then the measurements, which are turned into a stage with the 2017 American Academy of Periodontology and European Federation of Periodontology worksheet. Four sources can be ranked 24 ways; each ranking is scored against the specialists, and a ranking that agrees better replaces this one. A radiograph reading can only raise the stage. If asked why the measurements are last: they are the most complete evidence but are missing on many charts; on these synthetic charts 45 of 240 had none.
