Health Transformation Lab

Better health answers from AI, and better care for the millions of people who ask.

More than 40 million people a day ask OpenAI's assistant alone about their health (OpenAI, January 2026). Those answers shape whether people see a dentist or a doctor, what they ask for, and what care they get. AI developers such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Google improve what public benchmarks measure, and no public benchmark we found checks answers where medicine meets dentistry, or whether an answer helps someone get care. The lab builds those benchmarks in the open, so the answers, and the care that follows, can get better.

Three benchmarks

Each one is open, checked against experts, and reports possible harm to patients alongside accuracy.

Built, on synthetic records

PerioBench: better care for gum disease through more accurate diagnosis

Gum disease is written down at almost every dental visit, but in pieces that often disagree, so it is missed, can't be followed over time, and can't be linked to diabetes care. PerioBench scores how accurately rules and AI diagnose it from the whole dental record; agreement with gum specialists is how accuracy is checked, not the goal.

  • The whole record, combined by rules: 234 of 240 charts right
  • The note alone, a small trained model: 219 of 240
  • A bigger medical AI used as is: 185 of 240

Scored against the generator's truth on synthetic charts: not clinical performance.

OralHealthBench: where medicine meets dentistry

Twelve leading AI models average 81% on the dental licensing exam but 22% on real patient cases (GlobalDentBench, 2026). OralHealthBench asks whether they connect the mouth to the rest of the body: a medicine that makes a tooth extraction unsafe, or gum disease and diabetes. Each question is traced to published evidence, with public results so improvements are visible to everyone.

AccessBench: does the answer help someone get care?

An answer can be correct and still not help someone with no coverage, no car or no dentist nearby. AccessBench grades whether an answer works in the person's circumstances, with the same rubric and harm grades as OralHealthBench; an answer that delays urgent care is unsafe.

How the benchmarks help

A benchmark improves answers when AI developers can run it easily and trust what it measures.

  • Realistic questionsConversations graded by key-point rubrics, in the format health benchmarks already use.
  • Safety firstAnswers that could harm a patient are counted and reported, not only the right ones.
  • Open and cheap to runOpen data and one harness, with the cost per answer printed.
  • Shared resultsResults published and checked with clinical partners, so progress is easy to see.
